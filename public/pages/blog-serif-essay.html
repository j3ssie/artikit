Inside the five Artikit blog templates

J By j3ssieAuthor, Artikit

Every number in this post was counted on the pages themselves. None of it is placeholder text.

The set began with three links and one request: blog pages with a small header and footer, to reuse for writing documents. It now holds five templates. This post goes through how they were made, in the order the work happened.

Start from the reference, with a ruler

Each template was drawn after one published post. Before any styling, that post was opened in a 1440px window and its computed styles were read from the browser: the width of the text column, the size and leading of the body, the size and weight of the title. A short script then counted the characters on every full line.

The five agree more than they differ. One column, a title no larger than 54px, body text between 16.7px and 20px, and lines that run a little past 75 characters.

Keep the look, change the measure

“The 66-character line (counting both letters and spaces) is widely regarded as ideal.”

Robert Bringhurst, The Elements of Typographic Style

So each template keeps the ground, the faces and the furniture of its reference, and gives up some width. Every column was narrowed in steps and recounted until a line averaged about 70 characters. This page, drawn after a 700px column, is set at 592px and averages 71.

Write text that is true

A template full of filler teaches nothing about the template. So the sample text of each page is about itself: the narrative reports the measurements, the release post announces the files, the research note counts its own lines, and the playbook helps you choose between all five.

Check it like code

Every page went through the same list before it was added to the chooser. One box is still open.

What the reviewers caught
  • A title specimen that shrank on a phone while its caption still said 48px
  • A download box that fell below the fold in a 1280px window
  • A marker on a chart with no number beside it
  • Captions that ran to 96 characters a line, on a page about staying under 75
  • Section heads that would have fitted any research note

Redraw what looks wrong

The first charts in the research note were thin marks on a bare tinted frame, and they looked nothing like the figures in the post they were drawn after. They were rebuilt as plots inside cream panels, with axes, gridlines, hatched and solid columns, whiskers and a legend. The numbers did not change. How they were drawn did.

The five templates

To choose between them, read the playbook. To start writing, copy any of the files and replace what is inside its article element.
