70 characters a line at 1440px

A line of text can be too long to follow and too short to settle into. Robert Bringhurst put the comfortable range at 45 to 75 characters, with 66 as the ideal.1 The accessibility guidelines draw a harder limit.2

“Width is no more than 80 characters or glyphs (40 if CJK).”

Success criterion 1.4.8, Web Content Accessibility Guidelines 2.2

Pulled in from 78 characters to 70

The three posts this set of templates was modelled on run a little past the range: at a 1440px window they average 76, 78 and 79 characters a line.3 Each template keeps the look of its reference and gives up some column width, until a line averages near 70.

Column width is the lever. With the window fixed at 1440px, the column of each template was changed from 280px to 680px, and the count rose in a nearly straight line each time. On this page every 7.8 pixels of column add about one character, so the range of 45 to 75 maps to columns of 369px to 605px.

The first draft of this page sat at 600px and averaged 74 characters, at the very top of the range. It was pulled in to 560px, which averages 70 and leaves room for a wider face.

Counted at nine widths

A fixed column still shrinks on a small screen, so the question for this page is narrower. Does it stay between 45 and 75 at every window width?

The page was opened in Chrome at nine window widths from 320px to 1920px. At each width a script counted the characters, spaces included, on every full line of the body paragraphs. The last line of each paragraph was left out, since it is rarely full.

The function is short enough to print. The same one runs on this page: it draws the picture at the top and fills the row named This window.

In range from 480px up

From a 480px window up, the average stays inside the range. It is 55 characters at 480px and 70 from 640px on, where the column reaches its full 560px and stops growing.

Below 480px the page falls short. At 375px, a common phone width, a line averages 42 characters, and at 320px it averages 34. Those short lines come from the width of the screen, not from the measure. They were left alone: lifting them into the range would take type smaller than 17px, and on a phone the size of the text matters more than the length of the line.

No line passes 75

An average can hide its edges, so the full lines at a 1440px window were also counted one by one. There are 27 of them. The shortest holds 64 characters and the longest 74. None passes the top of the range.

What the result does not show

The count holds for one face, one browser and one language. Each of the three can move it.

  • One face. Source Serif 4 at 17px. A wider face fits fewer characters in the same column, and so does the fallback serif the page shows before the web font arrives.
  • One browser. Chrome on macOS. Other engines break lines a little differently.
  • One language. English prose. Longer words leave a more ragged edge and a lower count.
