Artikit has five blog templates. Each is one HTML file with a small header and footer, and each was drawn after a different published post. They hold the same kinds of block, so the choice is about register: how the page should sound before anyone reads a word.

If you are about to write something and are not sure which file to copy, this page is the short way to decide.

TL;DR

Start most documents on the release template. It is the plainest of the five, it is light first, and its rail lists the sections for you.

From there:

  • Move to the narrative when the document tells how something was made, in order
  • Move to the research note when the claim needs plots, a table and footnotes
  • Stay on this playbook when the reader has to choose or follow steps, since it is built around tables and code
  • Move to the essay when the text is long and personal, and a callout or a checklist helps it along

Pick a template by document

DocumentStart withWhy

A redesign or a build, told in orderNarrativeA centred title, an opening figure and figures stacked for comparison.

A how-to, a comparison or a decision guidePlaybookTables, code boxes and a tree of sections that follows the scroll.
An essay, a retrospective or a memoEssaySerif text at 20px, a callout, a checklist and a toggle.

The trade-offs

Three choices separate the five, and none of them is about what you write.

Sans against serif

Three of the five set their text in a sans face and two in a serif. The serif pages are easier to read at length and harder to scan, so the research note sets its table and its plots in sans, and the essay keeps its tables short.

A rail against a bare column

Four of the five carry a rail beside the text: the release post, the research note, the essay and this page. The narrative has none. A rail helps a reader who jumps around and distracts one who reads straight through. On a phone each rail moves above the text.

Light against dark

Every template has both schemes and follows the system setting until you press T or use the switch in the header. Only the narrative is drawn dark first. Choose by where the document will be read: dark suits a screen at night and washes out on a projector.

Start a document

  1. Copy the file and rename it, keeping the blog- prefix
  2. Replace what is inside <article class="post"> with your own text
  3. List each h2 and h3 in nav.tree, so the rail can follow the scroll
  4. Add the page to index.html and llms.txt, so the chooser lists it
