Three blog posts, measured

j3ssie14 November 2026

Three article pages were opened at 1440 by 900 and their computed styles read from the browser. This post records what they share, where they part, and which numbers the three templates in this set kept.

The three were a design narrative from Linear, a release post from Vercel and a research post from Anthropic. They were picked as references for a set of article templates, and the first step was to stop looking and start counting.

Each value came from a few lines in the browser console. Sites change, so read every number here as a snapshot of 14 November 2026.

One column, 624 to 684 pixels wide

None of the three puts a sidebar beside the text. The body sits in a single centred column: 624px on Linear, 684px on Vercel and 632px on Anthropic. In a 1440px window that leaves more than half the screen empty, on purpose.

Figures may run wider than the text. Linear’s opening image reaches 717px, Vercel’s screenshots 800px and Anthropic’s diagrams 848px. The text never follows them out.

“Anything from 45 to 75 characters is widely regarded as a satisfactory length of line for a single-column page set in a serifed text face in a text size.”

Robert Bringhurst, The Elements of Typographic Style

Counted on every full line of the first long paragraphs, the three posts average 76, 79 and 78 characters a line. All three sit just past the top of that range.

A title no larger than 48 pixels

Two of the three set the title at 48px and the third at 32.5px. None goes bigger, even with a whole screen to fill. What changes is the weight, the tracking and the alignment.

Section heads stay small too: 24px on Linear, 32px on Vercel and 21px on Anthropic. The space above a head is two to three times the space below it, so each head reads as the start of what follows.

Body text at 17 pixels, give or take one

Body copy runs from 16.7px to 18px, on a leading of 1.53 to 1.6. Captions drop to 12px or 13px and take a muted colour. That is the whole type system: a title, a head, a body and a caption.

What the templates kept

The three templates keep the single column, the wider figures, the small title and the short type scale. They change one thing, the measure. Each column is pulled in so that a line averages nearer 70 characters than 78.

The three templates

  1. Narrative, this page: Inter at 17px on a near-black ground.
  2. Release: Geist at 18px, with a rail for the date and the contents.
  3. Research note: Source Serif 4 at 17px, with footnotes and numbered figures.

The research note goes one step further and reports the line length of its own page at nine window widths.
